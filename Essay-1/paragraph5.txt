However, these signs of progression don’t come without equally haunting omens for both music consumers and artists. Due to the nature of DSPs and digital stores, consumers never truly own the music they stream as it only “creates a copy of the file…the listener does not get to keep the reproduced file; they can only listen to the audio by accessing it on their platform of choice”(CD Baby) meaning if a DSP decides within their own discretion to remove a song from their platform, listeners have little to no recourse to fight back beyond petitioning or demanding a refund. In the same way aggregators, especially those with large market shares of independent releases, have the full right, so long as it’s within their terms of services, to expropriate additional percentages from artists, remove or delay songs from digital stores without given justification, and even utilize artists catalogues in certain cases (TopMusicAttorney 6).
