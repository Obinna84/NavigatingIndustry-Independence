It was believed that with more faces that reflect the diversity of signed artists in executive boardroom positions, and ownership, that the policing and exploitation of creative expression of black artists would be less endemic to the operations of the music industry; however, stories of artists like J Cole seem to prove that the problem runs deeper than representation. Signing to Roc Nation in 2009, Cole sought to rapidly become an undeniable force in hip hop, dropping his second mixtape, The Warm Up, and featuring on Jay-Z’s (owner of Roc-Nation) upcoming album at the time, The Blueprint 3 on A Star is Born. This enthusiasm was quickly shot down after being told by Jay-Z and label executives that he couldn’t release a debut album until he had a certified hit song to go along with it (Moore). In a scramble to maintain the momentum generated from his last mixtape, Cole released another mixtape, later becoming a cult classic, entitled Friday Night Lights which featured the socially conscious and introspective tracks he had become known for like 2Face, See World, and Too Deep for the Intro, while also vocalizing his experience with his label on songs like Cost Me A Lot. Cole would eventually have a breakout single entitled Work Out and release his first studio album, The Sideline Story, in 2011, but the wrench in his plans of the album delay would be a pain in his side he recalled for the rest of his career climaxing in December of 2024 at his 2014 Forest Hills Dr. 10th Anniversary show following the long awaited release of his old mixtapes on DSPs (Griffin).
