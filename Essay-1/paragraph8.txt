Nevertheless algorithms aren’t perfect and with the increased freedom afforded to artists comes the toll of greater responsibility with fan interaction, image curation, and marketing. In an interview, Howard student and creative, StorME, touched on how she feels this increase in responsibility draws attention away from the creative process and the pressure on conforming to standards most rewarded by social media algorithms. “Sometimes people tend to focus more on the content than their music. So they’ll be like ‘Okay, we’ll use this chorus for the video’ or ‘This’ll be the line that makes the streams go up on TikTok…’ the verse will be four words, but the chorus is something that sticks, because they know that’s the sound the listeners going to use on that 15 seconds to make a dance to.” This sentiment of saturation was also echoed in another interview with K Hamid, Howard student and creative, stating “Because anyone can release music nowadays it has led to a lot of saturation of the industry… people who don’t really have a love for music, but a passion for money and fame are replicating the things they hear that are club bangers… creating thousands of clones and watering down the music industry”(Kabia). This phenomenon is now being dubbed, the TikTokification of music(Heuer) derived from the social media platform TikTok which has become a primary hub for music discovery in the 2020s.
