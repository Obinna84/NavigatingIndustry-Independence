Not only is there the pressure on artists to release more to recoup on the costs of distribution, but also the pressure to forgo or alter one’s unique voice to suit formulas shaped by social media algorithms. Social media’s like TikTok, Instagram Reels, and Youtube Shorts have become essential marketing tools for upcoming artists, as they’ve become a primary location for music discovery in an age where radio and blogs hold less weight (Trigger). These platforms are known for their short form content, favoring videos within a range of 10-30 seconds, which isn’t exactly conducive to showing off a full song, but rather a captivating intro sequence, or a catchy hook that can be danced to or used in an edit. Artists have spoken on the pressure of conformity this has on their music, and according to some has led to the devaluation of lyricism and impassioned messaging across genres (Elliot). In an attempt to name the phenomena, some have begun to call this problem the TikTokification of music (Heuer), dubbed after one of the largest social media platforms currently, and the most popular destination for short form content.
