On the surface level this is an amazing cost saving change for artists, as after only a certain amount of uploads the upfront cost of the subscription becomes cheaper than having to pay each time one releases. However, this comes with the tradeoff of pressuring artists to release more music than may be necessary in an attempt to recoup, saturating their catalogue with unpolished releases, and additionally with clauses like that of DistroKid and SoundCloud even the removal of all uploaded releases upon cancellation of subscriptions (TopMusicAttorney 4), effectively binding artists to a single distributor. While aggregators have mostly taken up subscription models for distribution purposes, they have not completely done away with the pay-per-release model either. Aggregators have now positioned themselves as one-stop-shop pseudo labels for independent artists, offering mastering services (some using AI), marketing and discovery tools, and legacy preservation, a bypass to their subscription cancellation clauses, increasing the overhead costs of distribution in the name of ease of access.
